# DISSCO synthesis benchmark family

Three large-scale `.dissco` pieces for benchmarking the LASS **synthesis**
pipeline (additive synth + Loudness + reverb + spatialize). They are built so
the LASS *render* — not the CMOD event-tree build — carries the cost and scales
~linearly with musical length.

| Piece | Duration | Sounds | Sound len | Synth audio | Partial-seconds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [`bench_1min.dissco`](bench_1min.dissco)  | 60 s   | 360 | 2 s  | 720 s    | 17 280  |
| [`bench_10min.dissco`](bench_10min.dissco) | 600 s  | 360 | 20 s | 7 200 s  | 172 800 |
| [`bench_30min.dissco`](bench_30min.dissco) | 1800 s | 360 | 60 s | 21 600 s | 518 400 |

"Synth audio" (sounds × sound-length) and partial-seconds scale **1:10:30**, so
the 10-min is ~10× the 1-min's synthesis work and the 30-min ~30×. Every leaf
Sound has 24 partials and hits all hot paths (additive synth, 24-band Loudness,
`REV_Advanced` comb+all-pass reverb, stereo spatialization, tremolo/vibrato).
~12 voices sound at once in every version.

Sound length is capped at 60 s (30-min) so peak RSS stays ~9 GB on a 15 GB box —
render memory scales with concurrency × sound-buffer (~0.16 GB per second of
sound length, measured); longer sounds OOM.

Generated by [`../gen_benchmark.py`](../gen_benchmark.py) — one place to retune
partials / sound count / reverb. Fixed `<Seed>` → single-thread renders are
bit-exact reproducible.

## Why count is constant and duration scales the sounds

The obvious design — scale the *number* of sounds with duration — fails, because
the **CMOD event-tree build is super-linear in event count**: 360 sounds → 14 s
build, but 3 600 sounds → 673 s build. A count-scaled 30-min piece (10 800 sounds)
would spend well over an hour in the serial CMOD build, burying the synthesis cost
we want to measure.

So the note-event **count is fixed at 360** and the synthesized audio is scaled
by making each Sound — and its enclosing s1 window — **longer** as the piece grows
(`sound_dur = duration/30`: 2 s → 20 s → 60 s). This keeps the LASS render as the
largest work item and lets it scale 1:10:30.

Note the CMOD build is *not* free even at constant count — it also grows with the
**timeline length** (it lays every event out in EDU across the whole duration):
~14 s at 1 min → ~516 s at 30 min. So build and render end up comparable on the
30-min. That is fine here: the `StageProfiler` reports them as separate stages, so
a synthesis change is measured on the **`render + join` / `partial synth`** stages
regardless of build cost. Keeping the count constant is what avoids the *count*-
driven build blow-up (673 s → ~a few minutes); the timeline cost is irreducible
for a genuinely long piece.

## Event tree

Mirrors the proven-valid `tutorial.dissco` template:

```
Top  (type 0, eventTop)      whole piece; 30 s1 children spread over the timeline
 └ s1  (type 4, eventBottom) window = 2·sound_dur; 12 sound children spread within
    └ sp1 (type 5, eventSound) -> one LASS Sound, 24 partials
```

Unlike `tutorial.dissco` (which stacks every top child at t=0), s1 events and
their sounds are spread across time, so the score buffer and voice density track
duration.

## Two fixes that make it work

1. **Reverb tail (piece).** The LASS decay tail is `T_r = -3·delay/log(alpha)`
   with `alpha = max(comb_gain/(1-lp_gain))`, and `sampleCount =
   getTotalDuration()·rate` where getTotalDuration *adds* the decay. The
   tutorial's `REV_Medium` + 0.5 s delay leaves `alpha≈1` → a hundreds-of-seconds
   tail → each sound allocates a ~450 MB buffer and OOMs at scale. These pieces
   use `REV_Advanced` with explicit comb/LP gains (`alpha≈0.561`) + 0.3 s delay →
   a musical ~1.6 s tail (audible reverb ~0.4 s), buffer ∝ the sound's own length.

2. **Spatializer leak (engine).** `Spatializer::spatialize_MultiTrack` leaked one
   `MultiTrack` per partial per sound (~36 MB/sound at 24 partials → 13.9 GB for
   the 1-min alone). Fixed in `LASS/src/Spatializer.cpp`; peak RSS 13.9 GB →
   0.97 GB, memory now bounded by the composite queue + score length, not sound
   count. **Rebuild if your `cmod` predates the fix:**
   `make lass config=release && make cmod config=release`.

## Running

```bash
# from repo root; output -> pieces/SoundFiles/<name>_0.aiff (24-bit AIFF)
echo 1 | ./cmod "$(pwd)/restructure/profiling/pieces/bench_1min.dissco"

# with the stage profiler (table to stderr, JSON optional)
echo 1 | DISSCO_PROFILE=1 DISSCO_PROFILE_OUT=stages.json \
    ./cmod "$(pwd)/restructure/profiling/pieces/bench_1min.dissco"

# thread-scaling sweep (rewrites <NumberOfThreads> in a scratch copy)
python3 restructure/profiling/benchmark.py \
    restructure/profiling/pieces/bench_1min.dissco \
    --threads "1 2 4 8 20" --reps 2 --seed 8675309 \
    --out restructure/profiling/bench_runs/sweep_1min
```

## Measured (i7-13705H, 8 threads, RTX 4050, leak-fixed build)

| Piece | Wall | CMOD build | LASS render | Peak RSS | AIFF (audio) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 min  | 31 s     | 14 s  | 16 s  | 0.43 GB | 15 MB (56 s)   |
| 10 min | 5.7 min  | 157 s | 184 s | 3.6 GB  | 146 MB (551 s) |
| 30 min | 17.6 min | 516 s | 541 s | 10.6 GB | 436 MB (1650 s) |

Render scales 16 s : 184 s : 541 s ≈ **1 : 11 : 34**, peak RSS 0.43 : 3.6 : 10.6 GB
— all fit a 15 GB box. Within render, **partial synth** and **Loudness** dominate
(156 s and 65 s summed across threads on the 1-min; 1827 s and 750 s on the 10-min);
reverb + spatialize are small slices. That is exactly the synthesis hot path the
restructure targets, so a synthesis speedup shows up directly on the `render +
join` / `partial synth` stages. Stochastic placement leaves the tail slightly
sparse, so actual audio is ~92 % of nominal duration.
